A live instrument for electronic music.

tracks. plays whole tracks, loops, a drum rack and a synth on one clock, and you play it from whatever hardware is on your table: a pad grid for the clips, a fader box as the mixer, a keyboard for the synth. It is made for DJs and live acts who play out, in every kind of electronic music.

Launch on the beat, not near it

Every clip starts and stops on the next bar or beat, whatever the launch quantum says. Tracks, loops, drum patterns and note clips sit side by side on one grid, and the same grid lies on your pad controller.

  • Sixteen slots per channel, a live loop from a quarter bar to eight bars, beat jumps that keep the phase, a capture button that turns the running loop into a new clip.
  • Every clip edit can be undone, also in the middle of a set.

Prepare a track, then play it

A whole track is not a loop. It plays from its start marker to the end and then stops, like a record. Anything up to sixteen bars, or what the analysis recognises as a loop, keeps going round. The clip editor is where you get a track ready for a set.

PrepareGrid
  • Cues and loops on eight pads. An empty pad saves the cue or the loop you just drew; a full one jumps there or switches the loop on. Auto suggests hot cues from the track.
  • Loops inside the track. A loop ahead of the playhead waits until the track runs into it, and saved loops can be moved and resized. In loop mode the track runs into each saved loop it reaches and turns there until you let it go on.
  • The intro is part of it. The start marker may sit before the one, so a track plays its intro and still lands on the beat. Right-click a transient and choose Set 1 to say where the one is.
  • Jump within a playing track, on the bar. Where the analysis needs help, warp markers, the grid and the warp mode can be set by hand.

Your library, in time and in key

Every track is analysed once: tempo, beat grid, downbeat, transients, key in Camelot notation and an energy curve. Then it plays in time with everything else, repitched, beat-sliced or stretched.

  • Next suggests what fits after the playing track: matching key, close tempo, and a step down, level or up in energy.
  • Browse and load while the set keeps playing.
  • Crates, grouping by genre, artist, key or tempo, and a player that prelistens on the cue output.
  • Stems: split a track into drums, bass, other and vocals (on the desktop), and switch them per channel while it plays.

Drums, notes and a synth of its own

A channel can carry a drum rack, a sampler or drift, the synth that comes with tracks. Patterns and melodies are clips like any other: they launch, loop and stop on the same grid.

Loops become instruments, too: slice one onto the sixteen pads of a new drum rack, with a pattern that plays it as before, so you can rearrange its hits. Or hand it to the sampler and play it in key.

An FX chain you play

Four FX channels sit in a row behind the faders: FX 1 into FX 2 into FX 3 into FX 4. Each one carries an effect, and every channel picks which of them it runs through. Pull a fader down and the echo keeps ringing.

Save what an effect is set to on one of its eight snapshot pads. Hold the pad and the snapshot takes over; let go and everything is back where it was. On a pad controller that sends pressure, pressing harder brings in more of the effect.

Bring your own controller

tracks. is not built around one device. Anything that sends MIDI notes or CCs can play it: pad grids, fader boxes, keyboards, DJ controllers. The screen is optional while you play.

A mapping is a short text file with one line per control, and every line is a command the app already understands. So a controller can do whatever the screen can, and all of them share the same selection.

# my-controller.map
name   My controller
device Controller   # part of the port name

# a whole clip grid in one line
pads session 8x8 note 36

# faders and knobs
cc 16 gain 1
cc 17 gain 2
cc 4  fx 1 filter freq

# hold a key, hold the effect
note 48 hold fxhold 1 echo
note 49 hold fxhold 1 tapestop
note 40 press transport

Learn, don’t type

Switch on learn, move a knob, pick what it should do from a menu. The editor in the settings writes the file, and your comments in it stay where they are.

Pad grids in one line

Session, scenes, hot cues, loops, drum pads or steps, with LEDs for clip colours, playing and queued. The common pad numberings are built in.

Hold, toggle, shift

Keys hold an effect, toggle a mute or fire any command. Shift and delete as modifiers, encoders as relative steps, several targets on one knob.

Starting points ship with the app: Ableton Push 1 (its own mode, with the display), Allen & Heath Xone:K2 (mixer, synth and effects layouts), Novation Launchpad Mini MK3, Akai APC40 and APC Key 25. Sync with other gear and apps through MIDI clock, in and out.

A back-to-back partner

Connect a language model to tracks. and you have someone next to you who knows the app inside out. Claude, Gemini or Codex, any client that speaks MCP will do. It works the way you do: through the same lines the screen and your controller send.

Tell it what you want, the way you would tell a friend at the decks. “A rolling bass under the track on deck two, leave some air in bar four.” It reads the key of the track, builds the sound, writes the clip and reads it back before you hear it. You listen and say what to change.

It does not listen to the room and it does not mix for you. It takes the work you would rather not do with one hand on a fader.

# you: a rolling bass under deck 2, A minor,
#      leave some air in bar 4
drift 3 new reese
key 3 from 2
midi 3 1 4
addnote 3 1 0.5 0.25 A1 0.9
addnote 3 1 0.75 0.25 A2 0.6
addnote 3 1 1.5 0.25 C2 0.8
# ...

# then it reads the clip back:
# 14 notes, all in A minor, offbeats,
# bar 4 rests after the first beat

Bass lines and patterns

Rolling, sparse, driving: notes in the key of what is playing, drum patterns and melody steps with chance and ratchet, as clips you can launch, edit or undo like any other.

Sounds from a reference

Give it a single note from a track. It measures bands, envelope and brightness, picks the closest drift preset and turns the knobs until the numbers line up. Your ears have the last word.

Your controller, set up

Plug in a pad grid and ask for a layout. It asks you to touch a key, learns which one it was and writes a mapping of its own. Your own mappings stay as they are.

FX chains

It sets up the four FX channels, sends the right channels through them and saves snapshots for the break, ready on your pads.

Hear it first

It can render a few bars to a file without touching the running set, so you can check an idea on headphones before it goes out.

You stay in charge

External control is off until you switch it on, and so is mapping. Every line it sends shows in the status bar, undo works as always, and saving or opening sessions stays with you.

One language for everything

Under the screen is a real-time engine in C++20 that never allocates or waits on the audio thread. On top of it sits a small text language. The screen, every controller mapping and saved sessions all speak it, so everything that can be done can be typed, mapped and saved.

A session is a script of these lines, and a reloaded session comes back exactly as it was saved. The engine is covered by a couple of hundred tests, including allocation checks on the audio path.

The same language is open to other programs. Switched on in the settings, tracks. listens on a local socket, and tracks --mcp makes it an MCP server for the partner above. It runs on your machine. The app has no chat and no account built in; the model lives in the client you pick.

# a channel with a drum rack and a pattern
rack 1 demo hard
pattern 1 1 16
steps 1 1 1 x...x...x...x...

# a track that knows its key, and a synth in it
load 2 1 "Peak Time.wav" auto
drift 3 new reese
key 3 from 2

# launch on the next bar, hold an echo
launch 1 1 4
fxhold 2 echo on
fxhold 2 echo off

Not released yet
